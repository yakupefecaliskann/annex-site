Evidence of accessibility work, not another scan report

The European Accessibility Act came into force on 28 June 2025. E-commerce providers are required to publish an accessibility statement. Annex is built for the agencies who have to keep that true across a portfolio of client sites.

What Annex does

Continuous scanning

Client sites are scanned on a schedule. Every time a site changes, its status is measured again — a scan from a year ago says nothing about today.

Platform-specific remediation

A finding is expressed in the language of the platform the site runs on: which file, which setting, which menu. Not an abstract criterion number, but a step someone can act on.

A dated record

Every scan is kept. Progress over time accumulates into a record that can be produced if anyone asks how the site is being maintained.

WCAG criteria

  • WCAG 1.1.1 — Non-text Content Every image that carries information needs a text equivalent; images that are purely decorative must be hidden from assistive technology.
  • WCAG 1.3.1 — Info and Relationships Structure you can see — headings, lists, tables, form labels — must exist in the markup too, not be simulated with font size and colour alone.
  • WCAG 1.4.3 — Contrast (Minimum) Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against its background; large text (18.66px bold or 24px regular and above) needs 3:1.
  • WCAG 1.4.4 — Resize Text Text must be able to scale to 200% without losing content or functionality.
  • WCAG 2.1.1 — Keyboard Everything a customer can do with a mouse must also be possible with a keyboard alone.
  • WCAG 2.4.2 — Page Titled Every page needs a unique title element that describes what the page is about.

What Annex does not promise

Annex does not guarantee compliance, and could not. Automated tests catch only a portion of accessibility issues; the rest needs human judgement. What Annex sells is that the work gets done on a schedule and that there is a record of it.
